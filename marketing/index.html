Capacity ratio vs frontier

One honest footnote — kept, not buried

14.3Q is topology × 7B capacity, i.e. how much addressable persona-capacity the matrix can express when every leg fires — not a single trained 14.3-quadrillion-weight file. The economical realization is shared base weights + cheap per-persona deltas, and only the active personas ever cost GPU. That is precisely why the bill tracks active-param-seconds and an idle matrix costs almost nothing.

One contract · one gate · one road

How the matrix works

No agent talks to another directly. A request enters through HOPE, is routed by APEX, checked by KNOLL, and only then reaches DREAM (simulate) or VISION (execute). Results return the same way. The separation of concerns is enforced in code, not suggested in a prompt.

Reads intent; the voice of the system. Interprets — never executes or creates.

The single legal road between agents. Calls KNOLL before every route.

Gates all traffic against six hard laws + a behavioral score. Allows or denies — never mutates.

Spins up, renders all possible outcomes, reports back via APEX, self-terminates.

Credibility is the moat

Big number, bigger receipts

We lead with 14.3 quadrillion because it's the true capacity of the topology — and we hand you the math, the formula, and the source file so it survives an engineer's first question. The number is the hook; the governance is the product.

Enforced, not advised

The capacity is only interesting because every parameter that lights up runs under a constitution: HOPE → APEX → KNOLL → DREAM/VISION, hashed per packet, gated by six hard laws, and billed on a tamper-evident ledger. HOPE literally cannot execute, VISION cannot govern, DREAM and VISION cannot talk. That's the difference between a headline and a system your compliance team will actually approve — and it's why the 14.3Q is a governed matrix, not a marketing slogan.
